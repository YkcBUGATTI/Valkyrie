NebulaProject Origins

The brief was settled in 2014: Adrian Newey with Red Bull and Aston Martin agreed to design a road-legal hypercar by the aerodynamic methods of Formula 1.

Codenamed AM-RB 001, revealed at Gaydon in July 2016 and named Valkyrie at Geneva in March 2017; first dynamic testing at Silverstone, with Max Verstappen and Alexander Albon driving.

AeroAerodynamics

Aerodynamics and thermal management were planned together from the first sketch. Scroll for the reason behind every aperture.

Aero · 01

The floor does the work

An open underbody and Venturi tunnels form the ground-effect core. Downforce reaches 18 kN at speed — about 1,800 kg.

Aero · 02

Cameras for mirrors

External mirrors are deleted; the side cameras feed the cabin's screen array, resolving drag and visibility together.

Aero · 03

Exhaust through the roof

The same layout as a contemporary Formula 1 car or the Porsche 918 Spyder; rear bodywork and cooling follow from it.

Aero · 04

The 8 mm negotiation

Chief designer Miles Nurnberger argued eight millimetres of extra cabin at one review — and the engineering team stood to applaud.

V12The V12

A Cosworth-built 6.5-litre naturally aspirated V12. No turbocharging. Redline 11,100 rpm.

The engine alone delivers 1,001 bhp at 10,500 rpm and revs to 11,100 — the ceiling for a naturally aspirated production road engine. Idle: 1,200 rpm.

The hybrid side is deliberately light: a Rimac 1.3 kWh KERS battery and an Integral Powertraction 160 hp axial-flux motor between engine and gearbox. Combined: 1,160 PS at 10,500 rpm, 900 N·m at 6,000 rpm. Reverse is performed by the motor — the Ricardo seven-speed single-clutch sequential has no reverse gear.

The engine weighs 206 kg dry and is a fully stressed chassis member, requiring no subframe; Cosworth designed it to run 50,000 miles between rebuilds.

Powertrain · Official Film
Powertrain

An acoustic record

From the Bahrain test: the camshaft gears work inches behind the occupants' heads; a 1,000 rpm idle was logged by testers as "savagely loud", and above 4,000 rpm with the windows down there is a genuine hearing-damage risk — active noise-cancelling headphones are the official recommendation. The external soundtrack, by the agreement of all three outlets, is the part of this car most worth recording.

Wheels & Brakes

Magnesium wheels, Cup 2 tyres

Magnesium wheels — 20-inch front, 21-inch rear, centre-lock. Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2, 265/35-ZR20 front and 325/30-ZR21 rear; the grippier Cup 2 R optional, both road-legal. Braking by Alcon and Surface Transforms CCM-R ceramics, 420 mm front and 385 mm rear discs, six-piston calipers.

Valkyrie AMR Pro
F·03

Valkyrie AMR ProTrack-only · not road legal

KERS deleted, the V12 alone remains — around 1,100 hp, redlining at 11,000 rpm. Eighteen-inch wheels carry Michelin LMP1-spec tyres; braking is F1-style carbon-carbon; air conditioning and infotainment are removed. Valkyrie LM
F·04

Valkyrie LMCustomer racer · announced June 2025

The customer racer built to mark the return to Le Mans, based on the AMR Pro and hardened for endurance racing: the same Cosworth lean-burn V12, capped at 520 kW (697 bhp) by regulation, redline 8,400 rpm. Regulation ballast and mandated electronics removed, the FIA tank enlarged, pump fuel accepted. Ten cars, each with the "Unleashed" programme of engineer-supported track days.

RecordThe Record

The first road-legal production car under two minutes — on road-legal Michelin Cup 2 tyres.

Silverstone GP Circuit · Production Car Lap Record

1:56.42

Darren Turner · Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 · ~200 mph down Hangar Straight

The previous record — a Manthey-prepared Porsche 911 GT2 RS — stood at 2:06.82.
The Valkyrie took more than ten seconds off it.

For scale: a Lamborghini Huracán GT3 Evo on slicks manages 1:58.165; a Formula Renault 2.0 single-seater does 1:56.052, just 0.37 s quicker; an F1 car, 1:27.097. The track that day still carried rubber from a bike meeting, with visible damp patches.

Le MansLe Mans

In 2025 the Valkyrie returned to Le Mans as the AMR-LMH — the first V12 prototype in top-class endurance racing in 14 years.

The team is The Heart of Racing: #007 and #009 in WEC, #23 in IMSA — numbers inherited from the Le Mans-winning DBR9s.

The AMR-LMH is rebuilt from the AMR Pro to regulations: the 6.5-litre Cosworth RA in lean-burn tune, hybrid hardware removed, output capped at 500 kW; an Xtrac seven-speed sequential; starting, generation and reverse combined in one starternator. Michelin slicks, 29/71-18 front and 34/71-18 rear.

2025 · WEC Hypercar

Debut in Qatar, finish at Le Mans

Global race debut at the 2025 Qatar 1812 km; that year's 24 Hours of Le Mans ended with both cars home, no mechanical failures — "We've arrived, we're present, we finished."
